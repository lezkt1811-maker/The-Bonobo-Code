# 04 — Human Female Social Networks

## Question

Have women's relationships with women functioned as **social infrastructure** (support, information, protection, cooperation)? What happens when those networks are dense, and when they are weak, fragmented, dispersed, privatized, or subordinated to another unit?

## Evidence (initial, to be expanded)

- 🟢 Nineteenth-century American "female world of love and ritual": intense, durable same-sex friendships and networks among middle-class women (Smith-Rosenberg 1975).
- 🟢 Voluntary associations formed a major arena of American women's public life (Scott 1991; Skocpol 2003).
- 🟢 Kin-based exchange networks in low-income Black communities as survival infrastructure (Stack 1974).
- 🔵 Presence of grandmothers and other kin associated with child survival in many populations, with variation by kin type and context (Sear & Mace 2008) **[verify]**.
- 🟢 In some societies, women's collective institutions were formally recognized (e.g., Iroquois clan mothers; Brown 1970) **[verify scope]**.

## Interpretation 🟡

Women have repeatedly built dense networks in many settings, and these networks appear to have provided material and emotional support. Whether they reliably converted into *collective power* is uneven and context-dependent.

## Alternative explanations

- Networks may be a *consequence* of women's segregation into gendered roles (sex-segregated labor, purdah) rather than a source of autonomy; segregation can both create networks and limit power.
- Historical sources overrepresent literate, propertied, and urban women.

## Contradictory evidence ⚪

- Women's networks can police women (gossip, reputation control, kin enforcement of norms).
- Competition and conflict among women (rivalry, exclusion, class and race hierarchy) are well documented.
- Male networks are also dense; no evidence yet that women's networks were *weaker* by comparison historically.
- Dense networks under strongly patriarchal institutions did not necessarily yield autonomy.

## The two guiding questions

1. *What happens when women have dense repeated relationships with other women?* Outcomes to check: health, survival, income, mobility, safety, political participation.
2. *What happens when those networks are weak, fragmented, geographically dispersed, privatized, or subordinated?* Compare across settings (see `05`).
