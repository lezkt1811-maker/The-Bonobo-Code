# 11 — Marriage, Singlehood, and Who Thrives Under What Conditions

## Question

> Who thrives under what circumstances?

Not "marriage is bad" and not "marriage is good." Heterosexual women are **not** presumed less autonomous.

## Variables

marriage · singlehood · friendship · social networks · relationship quality · economic security · loneliness · community · age · sexual orientation · gender · caregiving · autonomy

## Evidence to build (all **[verify]**)

| Claim under test | Starting sources |
|---|---|
| Married people, on average, show better health/longevity, with selection effects disputed | Waite & Gallagher 2000; Holt-Lunstad et al. 2010 |
| Marriage benefits differ by gender and quality; unhappy marriages are associated with worse outcomes | Bernard 1972; Gove 1972 (historical debates) |
| Single people, and especially single women, may have stronger friend/community ties than married people | DePaulo 2006; Sarkisian & Gerstel 2016 |
| Social connection outside romantic relationships buffers loneliness and health risks for singles | Holt-Lunstad 2010; loneliness literature |
| Economic security shapes single-women outcomes (wealth gaps) | Household wealth data |
| Caregiving burdens differ by gender and marital status | Caregiving studies |
| Sexual orientation modifies the above | LGBTQ health/family research |

## Interpretation 🟡

The likely structure is conditional: outcomes depend on relationship quality, resources, and the presence of non-romantic ties. The project-specific question is whether social connection outside romantic partnership changes outcomes for single women.

## Alternative explanations

- Selection: healthier, wealthier people marry (and stay married).
- Age/cohort effects.
- Income, education, and race confound both marriage and outcomes.

## Contradictory evidence ⚪

- Marriage advantages persist in many studies after controls (contested).
- Some single women report loneliness and economic strain.

## Open questions

- Do dense female friendship networks compensate for absence of a partner as much as a partner would?
- What is different for single mothers and older single women?
