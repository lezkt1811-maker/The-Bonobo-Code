# 16 — Women's Sports Teams and Workplace Networks

All citations general-knowledge, **[verify]**.

## Question

Statement #31–32 propose that women's teams create a female social environment many adult women lack. What does the evidence on sports and workplace networks actually show?

## Evidence

| Claim | Label | Source **[verify]** |
|---|---|---|
| Title IX (US, 1972) increased girls' sports participation substantially | 🟣 | Participation statistics (NFHS, NCAA) |
| Exposure to high-school sports opportunities from Title IX was associated with higher female college attendance and employment, including in male-dominated fields | 🔵 | Stevenson 2007; Stevenson 2010 *Review of Economics and Statistics* |
| Team participation is linked with better psychosocial outcomes in many studies; causality is hard to establish | 🔵 | Sports psychology reviews |
| Many girls drop out of sport during adolescence | 🔵 | Women's Sports Foundation reports |
| Women's professional networks (e.g., employee resource groups, mentoring circles) are widespread; evidence on effects is mixed | 🟡/🔵 | Management and sociology literature |
| Women often benefit from *sponsorship by high-status people*, who are often men, as well as from peer networks | 🔵 | Burt 1998; Ibarra 1992 |

## Interpretation 🟡

Teams are a good natural example of **proximity → familiarity → trust → cooperation**, because they enforce repeated contact around a shared goal. The Title IX results are notable because they are among the few quasi-experimental estimates tying a women's-participation institution to later outcomes. They concern *sports opportunity*, not specifically female network density.

## Alternative explanations

- Sports may build skills, confidence or health rather than networks.
- Women who play sports differ in resources and family support (selection).
- Title IX was a legal change with many other effects.

## Contradictory evidence ⚪

- Team environments can also involve rivalry, exclusion, body pressure and abuse.
- Networks of only same-sex peers may offer fewer high-status connections.
- Corporate surveys that note many senior women are former athletes are descriptive and subject to selection.

## Open questions

- Does adult recreational team sport (not school) create lasting friendship networks? Needs data.
- Which parts of team participation (repeated contact, shared goals, coaching, status) do the work?

## Sources **[all verify]**
