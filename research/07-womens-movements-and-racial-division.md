# 07 — Women's Movements and Internal Division

## Question

Have women's movements been weakened by divisions among women (race, class, sexuality, nationality, citizenship, religion, disability, age, marital status)? And where have women overcome such divisions?

Hypothesis chain (🔴):

```
women need collective power → women organize → inequalities among women enter the organization → women fragment → collective power decreases
```

> **Guardrail:** do not reduce this history to "Black women chose Black men and white women were selfish." The record is more specific and more contested than that.

## Interpretation 🟡

Inequalities among women are real and shaped who was included in movements and whose interests were prioritized. That is different from saying the movements failed *because of* those divisions; movements also faced repression, opposition, funding limits, and strategic disagreement.

## Alternative explanations

- External repression and backlash.
- Organizational design and leadership conflict.
- Different women had genuinely different interests, not only prejudice (e.g., labor protective laws that helped some and constrained others).

## Contradictory evidence ⚪

- Successful multiracial and cross-class alliances exist (to be documented case by case).
- Fragmentation into parallel organizations sometimes increased total activism.

## Open questions

- Under what conditions do coalitions across difference hold?
- Is fragmentation a cause of decline or a symptom?
