# 02 — Female Coalitions (Bonobo Comparative Case)

## Question

Do female coalitions in bonobos explain female social power, and what do they reveal about the limits of "female solidarity" as a concept?

## Evidence

### 2.1 Female coalition study (Communications Biology, 2025) 🔵

https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-07900-8

- About **30 years** of observations across **six wild bonobo communities**.
- Female coalition frequency was **associated with** greater female power relative to males.
- About **85%** of observed female coalitions targeted **males**.
- Researchers discuss coalitions in relation to male aggression toward females and infants.

**What this does NOT show:**
- That bonobo females "always defeat males."
- That coalitions *cause* power (association, not proven causation).
- That 85% male-targeting means female coalitions are generally directed at men; it describes coalitions as observed in these communities.

### 2.2 Female-directed coalition aggression — contradictory case (Scientific Reports, 2026) 🔵⚪

https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-40297-w

- A documented intergroup encounter involved a coalition **primarily composed of females** and resulted in **infant death**.
- Purpose in this project: prevents reading bonobos as a "peaceful female utopia." Female coalitions can be aggressive, including across groups.
- Details to confirm from full text: number of events, which group's infant, ecological context **[verify]**.

### 2.3 Chimpanzee vs. bonobo killing (Nature, 2014) 🔵

https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13727

- 18 chimpanzee communities, four bonobo communities.
- Substantially more killings recorded in chimpanzees than bonobos.
- Do **not** convert to "women are peaceful." The relevant question: how do different social structures affect aggression, coalition formation and violence?

## Interpretation 🟡

In bonobos, coalition is a mechanism, not a moral quality. Female coalitions can constrain male aggression and can also be used against outsiders. What the data suggest is that *sustained bonds among females create a capacity for joint action*. That is the piece relevant to the project's coalition-to-power chain; the moral valence is separate.

## Alternative explanations

- Ecological abundance, party size and female rank order could produce both coalitions and power.
- Male behavior (e.g., mothers backing sons) may modulate outcomes.
- Observer sampling: coalitions are conspicuous and may be over-recorded.

## Contradictory evidence ⚪

- Female-led aggression and lethal violence documented (2.2).
- Female power varies across communities; not universal.

## Open questions

- Which conditions turn female bonds into coalitions?
- Are coalitions primarily protective, or also competitive with other females?
