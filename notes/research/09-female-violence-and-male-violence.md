# 09 — Violence: Vulnerability, Protection, and Female Solidarity

## Question

> What characteristics of human social organization make women vulnerable to male violence, and what forms of female solidarity reduce that vulnerability?

Not claimed: "patriarchy causes all violence." Also in scope: female-perpetrated violence, group violence, and violence between women.

## Topics

intimate partner violence (IPV) · female homicide / femicide · violence against women · male violence against women · female-female violence · group violence · social isolation · protective networks · community intervention

## Interpretation 🟡

If abusers commonly isolate victims, then networks that make isolation harder could plausibly protect. That is a mechanism-level hypothesis. What is documented is that isolation is a *tactic* and a *correlate*, not that female networks reliably *prevent* violence.

## Alternative explanations

- Poverty, firearms access, alcohol, and legal enforcement are large drivers.
- Protective effects might come from any network (mixed-sex, kin), not female-specific ones.

## Contradictory evidence ⚪

- Networks can pressure women to stay in abusive relationships (family honor, religious norms).
- Female-perpetrated aggression and relational aggression are documented; women are not "naturally peaceful."
- Community intervention results vary by setting; several trials show null effects.

## Open questions

- Which specific female-network features (e.g., shelters, peer networks, kin proximity) reduce risk in causal studies?
- What is the marginal effect of female-only networks over general social support?
