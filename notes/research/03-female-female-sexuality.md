# 03 — Female-Female Sexual Behavior and Bonding

## Question

What is documented about female-female sexual behavior in bonobos and mammals more broadly, and what can (and cannot) be inferred about bonding and about humans?

## Evidence

### 3.1 Bonobo female-female sexual behavior 🔵

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31449811/

- **971 sexual events** analyzed.
- About **65%** involved female-female genital-genital rubbing.
- Female pairs more likely to remain **in proximity afterward**.
- Greater **oxytocin** increases after female-female interactions than after heterosexual copulation, **in that sample**.
- Female sexual interaction frequency was associated with **coalitionary support**.

**Be precise about what was measured:** behavior, post-interaction proximity, urinary oxytocin in a limited sample, and coalition patterns. It is a study of association.

**Do NOT claim:**
- researchers proved female sexual behavior exists *solely* for bonding;
- oxytocin proves bonding or emotion (oxytocin is a broad, context-sensitive hormone);
- results generalize to all bonobo populations or to humans.

### 3.2 Same-sex sexual behavior across mammals 🔵

PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/10547684/

- 2023 phylogenetic review documented same-sex sexual behavior in **261 mammal species** across many families; especially frequent documentation among primates.
- **Methodological point:** historical **underreporting** is likely because same-sex behavior was inconsistently recorded.
- Do not draw simplistic conclusions about human sexual orientation from animal behavior.

## Interpretation 🟡

In bonobos, sexual behavior is multi-functional and appears to be part of social regulation (tension reduction, affiliation, coalition). Female-female genital contact may contribute to social bonding in this species; that is a reasonable hypothesis with correlational support, not a demonstration of a single purpose.

## Alternative explanations

- Sexual contact may follow existing friendship (result, not cause) or reflect rank and tension management.
- Oxytocin release may accompany physical contact generally.
- Sampling of only certain individuals or contexts.

## Contradictory evidence / limits ⚪

- Sexual behavior in other species does not map onto human orientation, identity, or politics.
- Function in one species does not imply function in another.
- Human female-female intimacy encompasses non-sexual friendship, caregiving, romance and more; the bonobo data speak only to one narrow behavioral channel.

## Open questions

- Does non-sexual physical affection (grooming, touch) play similar roles across primates, including humans?
- What is the evidence base for touch and oxytocin in human female friendships? **[to research]**
