## Witch trials 🔴

Hypothesis: property, inheritance, gender, and women's social independence sometimes intersected with witch persecution.

**Do not conclude** "men burned women to steal their land." Examine:

- property, inheritance, widowhood;
- local disputes and neighbor conflict;
- gender of accused and accusers (many accusers were also women);
- role of church and state authorities and legal procedure;
- economic incentives, including confiscation practices in some jurisdictions.

What the scholarship is generally reported to show (**[verify all]**):

- Roughly 75–80% of accused in Europe were women, with variation by region; in some areas (e.g., Iceland, Normandy, Estonia) men were the majority (Levack; Apps & Gow 2003).
- Many accusations arose from local neighbor conflict, often between women (Briggs 1996; Macfarlane 1970).
- Elderly, poor, and single/widowed women were over-represented in some regions.
- Confiscation of property occurred in some jurisdictions but was not universal and did not drive most trials (Levack; Leeson & Russ 2018 argue competition between churches).
- The "witches were healers/midwives persecuted for their knowledge" narrative (Ehrenreich & English 1973) is **not well supported** by most later scholarship; midwives were seldom the targeted class, and many trial records do not center healing (Green 2008; Harley 1990) **[verify]**.
- Federici's *Caliban and the Witch* (2004) argues a Marxist-feminist thesis about primitive accumulation and control of women's bodies; it is an interpretive argument that historians have partly criticized **[verify]**.

## Interpretation 🟡

Independent women's institutions are documented across time, though many were subordinate to religious or state authority. Persecution of women occurred in many forms; single-cause explanations (property theft, healer purge) are not supported by mainstream scholarship.

## Contradictory evidence ⚪

- Institutions were often sponsored or supervised by male authorities.
- Survivorship bias.
- The witch-hunts were not unique to women.
