# 50-Sentence Conceptual Framework

## Conceptual Framework / Working Ideas

> **Status: 🔴 Hypothesis / 🟡 Interpretation.**
> These are the project author's conceptual statements. They are **not established facts** and must **not be cited as research findings**. They exist to generate testable questions. Each is meant to be checked against evidence in `research/` and `hypotheses/`.

---

1. Women need a social world made up of women, not just individual relationships with men.
2. What is missing is not necessarily a girlfriend, but a broader female social ecosystem.
3. Female friendship is only one part of a much larger system of female social connection.
4. Women can create collective strength when they have sustained relationships with other women.
5. A woman surrounded by other women has access to forms of support that a woman isolated inside a nuclear household may not have.
6. Female social networks can provide emotional support, practical assistance, information, protection, belonging, and collective power.
7. The ability of women to form strong relationships with one another may be an important source of female autonomy.
8. Women need places where relationships between women can become deep, durable, and consequential.
9. Female solidarity requires more than telling women to support each other; it requires environments where women can actually know one another.
10. Repeated proximity creates the conditions in which trust, friendship, cooperation, and coalition can develop.
11. The nuclear family concentrates a tremendous amount of social life inside a private household.
12. When women's primary social unit becomes the husband-and-children household, relationships between women can become secondary.
13. A society organized around isolated households can fragment relationships that might otherwise become collective networks.
14. The privatization of women's lives can reduce their access to broader female communities.
15. Social isolation can make individual women more dependent on the institutions and relationships immediately surrounding them.
16. Female independence is not only an economic question; it is also a social question.
17. A woman may have legal rights while still lacking the social infrastructure necessary to exercise those rights collectively.
18. Collective autonomy requires networks, not merely individual autonomy.
19. Women cannot exercise collective power if they are rarely given opportunities to form durable collective relationships.
20. The absence of female social infrastructure may itself be an important social problem.
21. The question is whether weakening women's networks also weakens women's bargaining power.
22. Strong female relationships can potentially create alternatives to dependence on individual men.
23. Women who can rely on one another may have more freedom to resist relationships or institutions that harm them.
24. Female networks can function as a form of social resilience.
25. Women supporting women can become more than emotional solidarity when that support produces tangible resources and protection.
26. Friendship can become coalition when people repeatedly cooperate toward shared interests.
27. Coalition can become collective power when relationships allow people to act together.
28. The important transition is from isolated women to connected women to organized collective capacity.
29. Female social power may emerge organically from dense networks of relationships rather than from formal political organizations alone.
30. The ability to organize collectively depends partly on whether people have opportunities to build relationships before they need them.
31. Women's sports teams are one modern example of women repeatedly gathering around a shared purpose.
32. A team can temporarily create the kind of female social environment that many women lack in ordinary adult life.
33. Digital communities may be recreating forms of female social connection that are difficult to find physically.
34. Women may use social media to manufacture community when their physical environments do not provide enough opportunities for connection.
35. The internet can allow women who feel isolated geographically or socially to recognize shared experiences in other women.
36. The recurring appearance of women-centered online communities suggests that the desire for female connection has not disappeared.
37. When one form of female community becomes unavailable, women may create another form somewhere else.
38. Female solidarity may therefore be less something that has to be invented than something that repeatedly re-emerges when conditions allow it.
39. The persistence of female networks despite social fragmentation raises the question of whether female coalition is a deeply recurring human social pattern.
40. The fact that women continually seek one another out may itself be evidence worth studying.
41. Bonobos provide a comparative case in which female relationships have substantial consequences for social power.
42. Female bonobos demonstrate that relationships among females can form the basis of coalitions rather than merely companionship.
43. Bonobo society raises the possibility that female relationships can have structural consequences for the distribution of power.
44. The relevant lesson from bonobos is not that humans should become bonobos, but that female-centered social organization is biologically conceivable in a close human relative.
45. The Bonobo Code asks what becomes possible when female relationships are treated as foundational social relationships rather than peripheral ones.
46. The research is ultimately about the relationship between female connection and female collective power.
47. It asks whether women have been deprived not simply of individual freedoms, but of social structures through which women can support and empower one another.
48. It asks whether rebuilding female social infrastructure could change women's autonomy, safety, resilience, and collective capacity.
49. The central problem is not simply that women are lonely, but that women may lack a sufficiently dense social world of other women.
50. The deepest question is whether a society in which women are genuinely connected to one another would produce a fundamentally different distribution of social power.

### Points where a statement needs a caveat before it can be tested

- **#12, #13, #14:** these assume network loss is caused by household form. Household form correlates with many other things (urbanization, wage labor, mobility). See `research/05`.
- **#42:** "demonstrate" is stronger than the bonobo data warrant; the evidence associates coalition frequency with female power in specific communities. See `research/02`.
- **#38, #39, #40:** re-emergence and "seeking one another out" could also reflect ordinary human sociality generally, not something specific to women. Needs a male-network comparison.
- **#49:** "lack a sufficiently dense social world" is a measurable claim about network density; needs survey/network data (see `evidence/datasets.md`).
