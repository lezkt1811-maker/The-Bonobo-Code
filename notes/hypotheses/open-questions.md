# Open Questions

## Definitions and measurement

1. What exactly counts as a **female social network** vs. a set of individual friendships? Density? Multiplexity (ties serving many functions)? Durability? Independence from men/institutions?
2. How do we measure **collective power** without circularity (defining it by the outcomes we hope to see)?
3. What is **autonomy** here: decision-making, mobility, economic independence, exit options, freedom from coercion? Different measures may give different answers.

## Causal structure

4. Does network structure cause autonomy, or does autonomy build networks, or both?
5. Are the claimed effects **specific to women's ties to women**, or general benefits of any social support? (Needs male-network and mixed-sex comparisons.)
6. Does the nuclear household *reduce* women's ties to other women, or do wage labor, mobility, and urbanization do that, with household form a side effect?

## Comparative

7. What can bonobo data tell us given one species, specific ecology and dispersal patterns (females disperse; males stay), and limited communities?
8. Which human societies have strong female networks *and* patriarchal outcomes, and what does that imply?
9. Do cross-cultural residence patterns (matrilocal / patrilocal / neolocal) predict women's status independent of other variables?

## Digital and contemporary

10. Do women-centered online spaces produce mutual aid, offline friendships, or coalition, or mainly parasocial belonging?
11. What are their costs: polarization, exclusion, harassment, dependence on a charismatic host, algorithmic volatility?
12. Why do participants perceive male participation as disruptive, and what do participants themselves say? (Requires interviews, not inference from watching.)

## History

13. What does scholarship actually support about witch persecution, property, and gender? (Hypothesis, not conclusion.)
14. Which women's institutions were autonomous vs. sponsored by church/state/men?

## Violence

15. Which forms of female solidarity demonstrably reduce vulnerability to male violence, and in what settings? (Community mobilization evidence is mixed and setting-dependent.)

## Ethics/method

16. How do we avoid a confirmation-biased reading list? Proposed: for every supportive source, log one skeptical source.
