# Proposed Revisions to the Hypotheses (from research 12–16)

These are *proposals*, not changes to `core-hypotheses.md`. All underlying sources are **[verify]**. Accept, edit, or reject each.

| Hypothesis | What the new research suggests | Proposed rewording | Source file |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 Fragmentation | Decline in close friendship is documented for both sexes, steeper for men on some measures | "Women's network-based resources (support, childcare, information, collective capacity) have declined relative to need in some settings and periods" — and test against men | `14` |
| H2 Network autonomy | Intimate and instrumental networks work differently; same-sex ties may carry fewer high-status contacts | Split into H2a (support networks → resilience/wellbeing) and H2b (instrumental/political networks → power and access) | `14`, `16` |
| H3 Coalition opportunity | Cases show pre-existing recurring contact made mobilization faster, but triggers and context matter | Add the condition "shared roles or interests" to proximity → coalition | `15` |
| H4 Infrastructure | Cooperative-breeding evidence gives a mechanism (shared childcare) independent of the bonobo comparison | Keep, with childcare as the lead testable domain | `13` |
| H5 Re-emergence | Recurring forms are real but male networks recur too; many were sponsored | Requires the male comparison before it can count as supported | `14`, `15` |
| H6 Collective power | Female power in mammals arises by at least three routes; bonds are neither necessary nor sufficient | State explicitly: bonds may *contribute* to power where the coalition route operates | `12` |
| H7 Internal fragmentation | Case studies include state capture and norm-policing, not only inequality among women | Add "capture and norm-enforcement" as competing explanations | `15` |

## Newly registered contradictory evidence (add to `contradictory-evidence.md`)

- C12: Female-dominant species exist without strong female bonding (some lemurs) and with intense female competition (hyenas). `12`
- C13: Kin effects on child survival are mixed; paternal kin sometimes neutral or negative. `13`
- C14: Men's friendship decline is steeper than women's on some measures. `14`
- C15: Same-sex networks may yield fewer instrumental/career returns. `14`, `16`
- C16: Microfinance group structure produced modest average empowerment effects. `15`
- C17: Title IX effects are about sports opportunity, not network density; selection and other legal changes confound. `16`

## Method note

The most useful single addition would be a **systematic** comparison of women's collective-action episodes (successes and failures), rather than cases chosen because they illustrate the idea. See open question in `15`.
