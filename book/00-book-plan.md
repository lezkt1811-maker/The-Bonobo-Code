## 1. Revised core thesis

**Blueprint version:** Humanity got trapped in a male-centered, patriarchal framework that severed women's natural bonds and erased the mechanics of female pleasure. Women can reclaim social power, deep solidarity and "heaven on earth" physical pleasure.

**Revised version (what the book can defend):**

> Women's relationships with other women are an under-examined source of social support, practical help, information, and collective capacity. Their availability depends on conditions we can describe (housing, work, mobility, institutions, digital spaces), not only on individual choices. Separately, women's knowledge of their own bodies, including sexual anatomy and pleasure, has been neglected in science and in culture. The book argues that both gaps are worth closing, and that they are different questions with different evidence.

Two tracks run through the book:

- **Track A — Social infrastructure.** Female networks, coalitions, collective power. Largely sourced in `research/`.
- **Track B — Bodies and pleasure.** Anatomy, sexual response, shame. Partly sourced; several claims still need primary sources (see §4).

Keeping the tracks separate stops Track B's evidence from carrying Track A's conclusions, and the reverse.

## 2. Ground rules carried over from the repo

- Bonobos are a **comparative case, not a blueprint.** No chapter says humans should or would organize like bonobos.
- Not "women are peaceful." Not "men are bad." Female coalitions can be violent (`research/01`–`02`, C1 in `hypotheses/contradictory-evidence.md`).
- "Patriarchy" must be unpacked into a **named mechanism** each time it is used (co-residence rules, property law, labor markets, violence, ideology).
- Every major claim gets a label and a source, or is marked as unsourced.
- Each chapter includes a **contradictory evidence** section. This is what keeps the book credible to skeptical readers, which is the blueprint's own stated goal.
- Do not claim: female sexuality has one purpose; all women bond with women or need women-only spaces; heterosexual women are less autonomous; evolution is destiny.

## 4. Changes from the blueprint (and why)

| Blueprint claim or phrasing | Problem | Change in the book |
|---|---|---|
| "Matriarchal cousins" | Bonobo females have influence through coalitions in some communities; "matriarchy" is a contested term and overstates the structure | Use "female-influenced" or "female-coalition" societies; define any term used |
| "Why bonobos don't suffer the patriarchy" | Implies bonobos escape male dominance. Male aggression toward females is documented (`research/02`) | Retitle to describe what female coalitions do and where they fail |
| "Human culture leaned into the wrong primate model" | Normative and a comparative-case-as-blueprint argument | Remove. Present chimpanzee and bonobo contrast as data, with caveats |
| "Penetrative sex alone fails ~80% of women for climax" | No source in repo. Survey figures vary a lot by method and definition | Replace with the survey literature, reported with ranges and definitions **[verify]** (e.g., Herbenick et al. 2017 **[verify figure]**) |
| "Fully mapped 3D in the late 1990s/2000s" | Plausible (O'Connell et al., 1998, *J Urol*; MRI work in 2005 **[verify both]**) | Keep, cite primary papers, check dates |
| "Multi-orgasmic potential without a refractory period" | Overstated. Evidence is limited and definitions differ | State as "some women report multiple orgasms; the evidence on frequency and mechanism is limited" |
| Oxytocin, dopamine and endorphins "flood" during intimacy | Not measured in the claimed way. Oxytocin is context-sensitive (`research/03`) | Report only measured findings; name what is not known |
| "Heaven on earth" | An experiential claim, not a finding | Keep as the author's voice in the closing argument; not in the science chapters |
| Sex at Dawn and similar books as models | Sex at Dawn is contested in the primatology and anthropology literature | Cite its arguments and critics side by side |
| "Patriarchy erased women's bonds" | Needs a mechanism; the repo's H1 says the decline is not yet shown to be female-specific | Use the mechanism-first wording from `hypotheses/core-hypotheses.md` |

## 6. Open decisions for the author

1. **Publishing path.** Essays first, then the book, is recommended. Each chapter's Track B claims need primary sources, and essays give readers and reviewers a chance to check them before the full text is fixed.
2. **Sexual content level.** Anatomy and pleasure can be discussed frankly and with care. Explicit description is a separate choice and should be made before drafting Ch 3 and Ch 5.
3. **The TikTok observation.** `notes/working-notes.md` says not to record identifying details. The book should describe norms in general terms, or leave it out.
4. **Title.** "The Bonobo Secret" implies a hidden fact the book then has to deliver. The repo's name, "The Bonobo Code", is more accurate to the research.
